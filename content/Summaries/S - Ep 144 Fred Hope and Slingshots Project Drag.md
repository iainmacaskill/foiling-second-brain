# S - Ep 144 Fred Hope and Slingshots Project Drag

[[Fred Hope]] (of [[Slingshot]], based in [[Hood River]] / the Gorge) returns for a ~2-hour deep dive on **Project Drag** — Slingshot's collaborative board-and-mast programme built around a Tuttle-mast connection. The hosts have real skin in the game: [[Liam]] built a near-identical board off the same CAD file and has ridden the track version for six months, and Fred sent him a Project Drag Tuttle sample to compare.

## In brief
Project Drag is Slingshot handing the reins to outside shapers — [[Ken Adgate]], [[Kyle Touhey]] and a box-builder (transcribed "Michael"/"McKinley," name uncertain) — to bring a proven Maui board/Tuttle setup to production. Fred talks through the origin, an unusually transparent development process (a public Discord, factory photos), and the core engineering trade-off: a Tuttle ("total") connection gives lower drag, stiffer feel and dramatically better high-speed touchdown recovery, but less adjustability and harder assembly — so it's aimed at the 10–20% chasing the ultimate setup, while 80–90% should stay on plate/track.

## Key topics & claims
- **Origin (Fred):** credit goes mostly to Ken Adgate, Kyle Touhey and "Michael." Ken shapes ~50 boards a year for friends "not for the money"; he offered his learnings to Slingshot to get more people on the product. Kyle handles Shape3D CAD; "Michael" builds Tuttle boxes in-house. Slingshot compressed its usual 12–18-month dev cycle to ~6 months out of excitement.
- **Radical transparency:** a **Discord** (~370 members, only lightly promoted) lets consumers interact directly with designers (Gabe/Bobby are on it), see factory photos, and read AI-generated summaries of design meetings per channel. Fred frames it as community-building — countering how Facebook groups and Kiteforum have become unfocused/spam-ridden (Kiteforum also plagued by hacking). [[Freddie]] adds that every brand he's dealing with is now chasing exactly this "community and inclusion."
- **Tuttle vs plate/track (the core tech):** the Tuttle head is **15.95mm** thick and the fibres run straight into the board, so it's stiffer and lower-drag. Fred's standout claims: better **high-speed touchdown recovery** ("it bounces back up onto foil") and cleaner glide. Downsides: shims, top-mounted screws, harder to disassemble, only ~2 inches of fore/aft adjustment, no rear inserts (so not for jumpers). Verdict: ~80–90% of riders should keep a plate; Tuttle is for the last 10% of performance.
- **Standardisation play:** Slingshot paid for the molds so "Michael's" Tuttle box could go into their factory and be sold to *other* brands (Hydrocraft / Matt Alsasser already buying) — the trade being it frees him to keep innovating. Goal: avoid yet another proprietary connection that alienates customers, echoing the earlier ONELOCK-from-Phantasm switchover.
- **Mast thinness vs stiffness (major thread):** thinner masts (11.8/12mm) glide better but can't match a thicker mast's stiffness — carbon gets brittle at the extreme. Fred relays [[Adrian Roper|Adrian]]'s (Axis) forum point that a mast tapering wider than ~15mm to meet a Tuttle head creates a weak point, so Tuttle suits riders for whom glide (not maximum stiffness) is paramount. Slingshot targets HMS-level stiffness, uses a titanium/metal collar in the Tuttle mast (sanded flush to cut drag), and is developing a **17mm** mast (Bobby still tuning) whose thickness makes it stiffer than any Tuttle-compatible mast can be. QC pain point: masts came out with thickness variance (~11.5–13.9mm) they had to tighten.
- **Liam's direct comparison:** he asked Ken & Kyle to help him build another wingboard and was sent essentially the production Project Drag file — his DIY "green machine" is a near-twin. Riding Fred's Tuttle sample (deliberately an early, not-yet-stiff one) he still found the **lack of drag on take-off/touchdowns immediately noticeable**, and stresses the touchdown *recovery* — not raw speed — as the real benefit; a plate is still "a good connection."
- **Back-to-back mast test (Liam):** compared **[[F-One]] 12mm vs 14mm** masts and Slingshot plate-HM vs Tuttle (V2 Eagle 790 vs Glide 725). In powerful easterly bumps he found the 12mm gave *too much* speed/glide (wanted a smaller foil); the efficiency gain was most obvious at **low speed / when recovering**, not across the whole range.
- **Which brands adopt Tuttle next?** Fred guesses downwind/race-focused brands first. [[F-One]] (Julien) and [[Armstrong]] ride the mast far forward, which fights Tuttle's limited adjustment; Axis's tempo mast (polished thin by "Frank") and [[Kane De Wilde|Kane]]/KT are candidates.
- **The human side:** Fred on managing a *friendship* not a business relationship (Ken/Kyle/"Michael" want no money — Ken asked only that a board be donated to a cancer raffle, "KEIPA"[?]; Kyle wants demo support); [[Jeff Logosz]]'s development spend and "blood pressure"; skew management; [[Mikes Lab|Mike Zajcik]] and [[Johnny Heineken]] chipping in via WhatsApp; raw long-form YouTube designer interviews (Edo, Trent). Boards retail ~$1,800.
- **Prone crossover:** smallest Project Drag is 50L (a 35L requested on Discord); Trent & [[Edo Tanas|Edo]] have been proning it on Oahu. Liam is sceptical high-rocker boards suit prone surf.
