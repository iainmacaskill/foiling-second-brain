# S - Ep 003 a delve into Liam and Freddie's winging careers

Recorded in [[Liam]]'s living room (duvets pinned to the wall to damp the echo after listener feedback). The hosts tell their winging backstories — the discipline the whole show was started to talk about — plus a listener Q on wake/boat-foil gear, and they tease the first run of guests.

## In brief
The episode splits into three: (1) a detailed answer to a listener's wake-foil gear question, drawn from [[Freddie]]'s summer learning to foil behind boats; (2) each host's full winging progression — what they learned on, the gear they burned through, their mistakes and their favourite sessions; and (3) a run-down of the first guests they've lined up. Both hosts converge on the same lesson: don't drop board volume and foil size too fast, and buy gear for what you're actually trying to achieve.

## Key topics & claims
- **Wake / boat foiling (Freddie):** three factors decide gear choice behind a boat — (1) **boat size / wake energy** (he's ridden a small rib, his dad's 19ft inboard "baby Mastercraft" ski boat, and a friend's powerful 28ft Axopar dubbed "HMS Basher"); (2) **skill level** — good riders position on the wave and pump so well they could "carry on with no wake"; (3) **riding style / speed** — most people wake-foil around 10 knots (pre-planing), where boats plough a good-sized wave. He runs the [[F-One]] Phantom **1480** behind the small ski boat and would drop to the **1080** behind a bigger wake; notes inboard props feel safer than staring at a big outboard.
- **Teaching his dad (Freddie):** bought a **[[Slingshot]] WF2** (~4'10", ~20–25L) with a half-strap so a total beginner has something to hook a front foot into without being trapped; strong caution against two tight straps (ankle-twist injuries — he's damaged his own).
- **Freddie's winging journey:** learned around **late 2020**; works in the industry (distributes [[Ozone]] kites in the UK and reps [[AXIS Foils|Axis]] foils). First glide came at a demo/instructor course run down by **Andy Gratwick** (~2020), with "Richie B" running circles around everyone. He was ~82kg on an **SIC 115L** board, a big **Axis** beginner front wing (~2000cm²), and an **Ozone Wasp 5m**. He then "dropped down too quickly" — a ~1500cm² pump-and-glide on an ultra-short fuse (which hurt his gybes/pitch control), then an **80L SIC Raptor**. Now winging on the **Axis ART 999** high-aspect wing. Admits he under-practised because he'd choose kiting whenever it got windy, and cramped up badly early on until he adapted (harness helped).
- **Liam's winging journey:** first session **autumn 2020**, sent out by his boss **[[Steph Bridge]]** (of Edge, Exmouth) "wind-with-tide" on an **[[F-One]] Swing 5m** on a Tarifa-made custom board (~90–100L, originally shaped for [[Guy Bridge]] to SUP-foil). Because he'd already kitefoiled, he "knew how a foil should ride." First session was a disaster — he drifted a mile down Exmouth beach — but it "clicked straight away" on session two in flat water behind the sandbar. His first own kit was an **RRD** 5m wing + RRD universal foil (~1550) on a converted formula-kite raceboard; he later fell for the **F-One Phantom 1080** (tried Steph's setup) and rates it as one of the most accessible foils. He built his own board (~90L, 5'2", a full-carbon copy of the **Ride Engine Moon Buddy**, drilled with holes to save weight). Rides front-strap-only after an ankle injury.
- **Shared philosophy / "willy-waving":** both push back on the industry hype of ever-smaller boards and foils. Liam relays a call with **[[Adrian Roper|Adrian]]** (Axis) about customers buying too-small boards, then wanting bigger ones two months later; Freddie, as the rep selling that gear, admits smaller boards were "good marketing." Advice: maximise your current gear before stepping down, and keep a crossover period with two boards.
- **Style debate:** Liam wants a critical, short-board surf style (turning hard, generating speed in the pocket); at [[Bantham]] they ride with **[[Rich Boughton]]** ("Richie B" / [[Kitebarn]]), described as "the endless summer of wing foiling" — drawn-out, controlled carves. This drives their disagreement over high-aspect wings (e.g. the Eagle) vs playful ones.
- **First pump-foil session:** Liam (with "Steezy Pete") tried it this week — hilarious, managing about one and a half board-lengths — on a **Slingshot 1150** pump-and-glide; they reference the newer **1310** and Hugo Wigglesworth's ~17-minute pump-foil record in New Zealand.
