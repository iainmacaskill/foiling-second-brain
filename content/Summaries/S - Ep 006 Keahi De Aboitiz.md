# S - Ep 006 Keahi De Aboitiz

[[Freddie]]'s first-ever solo interview ([[Liam]] was away in France) — a chat with 4x world-champion wave/surf-style kite rider [[Keahi De Aboitiz]], recorded in the [[Cabrinha]] airstream at the Hayling Island BKSA kite event during the brand's South Coast UK tour. Set up by Karl Cadwallader of [[Cabrinha]] UK (per the intro, also thanks to a Cabrinha marketing manager whose name is garbled in the transcript). Freddie frames it as nerve-racking, unpractised early-format territory and admits to being hungover.

## In brief
Keahi — Australian by birth, now largely Hawaii-based (his mother is Hawaiian, giving him a US passport; he moved there more or less full-time once COVID made the Australia commute pointless) — talks through his path from kite racing into kite foiling around 2012 in Maui, his long [[Cabrinha]] sponsorship, his current foil/board/wing quiver, and a growing interest in downwind foiling. The tour itself (Germany, Holland, France, then the UK) and UK-specific talking points (seaweed, spot names, the tiny-board trend) run alongside the gear talk.

## Key topics & claims
- **Background (Keahi):** Australian, mother from Hawaii, American passport; moved to Hawaii close to full-time roughly 10–11 years ago and permanently once COVID hit; girlfriend also based there.
- **Kite foiling origins (Keahi):** first tried a kite foil almost by accident on a no-wind day in Maui around 2012 (the year of his first world title) — "got my ass handed to me," took a few hours to link it together, then got hooked. Credits [[Kite Foiling]]'s wider explosion to a rival brand ("Go Foil," no existing page — see candidate below) making the first thicker, less-efficient foil aimed at small-wave riding rather than performance; recalls his own early attempt towing into a wave on a Cabrinha "Double Agent" foil as inefficient by comparison. Notes that Go Foil has apparently never had a UK distributor.
- **Cabrinha career (Keahi):** sponsored internationally by [[Cabrinha]] since roughly 2011 (~11 years at the time of recording), nationally in Australia before that. Was briefly with Neil Pride for foil gear before switching back to Cabrinha for everything foil-related "a few years ago."
- **High-aspect vs medium-aspect foils (Keahi, echoing a running Freddie/Liam debate):** Cabrinha's high-aspect "ART" line has "taken over" his riding the last year or two; medium-aspect foils now feel "slow and sluggish" to him for pumping/gliding, though he concedes they still suit steeper, tighter-pocket waves where the extra drag helps with tight turning and stops you outrunning the wave. He tells Freddie he's "completely comfortable" with Freddie telling Liam to write off low/medium aspect for pumping and gliding.
- **Current quiver (Keahi):** foils — Cabrinha H-series (650/800/1000/1200, developed with a teammate named "Brody," transcribed only as first name), mostly rides the 800, uses the 1000 in small/light-wind waves and the 650 for towing; was also riding an unreleased ~700 sample at the event. Wings — Cabrinha's X-series (four sizes, all sharing a 90cm span, chord increasing down the range for user-friendliness); a high-aspect X-series mark 2 is coming, closer to the 1200 H-series geometry. Boards — Code range (beginner/intermediate, down to a 4'8"/58L he uses as a light-wind board, and a 5'5"/78L he was riding while returning from a knee injury); a newer, smaller "Links" range at 4'3" (37L), 4'5" (44L, his usual go-to at 80kg) and 4'7". Says a modular fuselage/tail system (shims, swappable tails, a "one-piece" efficient tail, and an advanced short-fuse setup he calls something like "Collideos," name uncertain) lets him retune maneuverability vs drive for conditions.
- **Discipline preferences (Keahi):** condition-dependent — surfing when it's pumping, kiting when there's wind with waves, wing foiling in lighter/lull conditions or when wave count matters most (wing foiling is, in his words, closest to tow foiling and lets you catch a wave "30 seconds before it breaks"). Does relatively little prone foiling now by comparison, and describes himself as still fairly new to downwind foiling.
- **Downwind foiling (Keahi):** growing personal interest via tow-assisted open-ocean swells and a couple of wing-foil downwinders; describes [[Hood River]] as an especially good spot because outgoing tide running against the wind stacks the swell into bigger bumps, and mentions [[AXIS Foils|Axis]] and [[Armstrong]] downwind board samples turning up there. Technique tip: unlike wave foiling (where you chase the steepest part of the wave you're on), downwind linking means scanning for the *next* bump behind the one you're on, since it will draw water from the one in front — "heads on a swivel." References [[James Casey]]'s prone downwind paddling background and a reported 200km+ world-record paddle that reportedly started with pure flat-water pumping.
- **Pumping technique (Keahi):** learning curve involves not overworking it — beginners tend to over-pump and, on high-aspect foils especially, that can actually slow you down. Tips: use the upper body/arms early on to help generate lift, shift front-foot weight slightly further back for better balance on a back-foot-heavy stance, and aim for fewer, more solid pumps that let the foil glide rather than constant thrashing.
- **UK tour observations (Keahi):** impressed by the standard of UK foilers, particularly at Poole, and by a Devon crew nicknamed the "KFC" (Colonel Foil Crew, said in jest) around what sounds like Croyde/Bantham/Bigbury (place names garbled in the transcript). Comments on UK seaweed being unusually sticky and hard to shake off a foil. Notes the UK trend of riders instinctively sizing boards down to match what they see Hawaii riders posting on Instagram, which backfires in the UK's typically lighter, gustier wind.
- **Other topics:** Cabrinha's co-branded "Drifter" beer with a South Carolina brewery, funding a programme that teaches injured/disabled military veterans to kite and foil; a UK launch tie-in with a Cornwall brewery (transcribed "Blunt Rock," uncertain) timed to the tour. Mentions duck-starting and boat-wake riding (including riding many wakes back behind a sternwheeler paddle steamer at Hood River) as another accessible way to get foiling time without waves or wind.

## Notable quotes or takes
- On high-aspect foils dividing opinion: after a year on Cabrinha's ART, riding a medium-aspect foil left him feeling it was "slow and sluggish" (paraphrased) — though he still rates medium-aspect for tight, steep-wave turning.
- On downwind bump-reading: described it as almost the reverse of normal wave foiling — instead of chasing the steepest section under you, you're already watching for the next bump behind it, because it draws water off the one you're on (paraphrased).
- On barrel-riding on a foil (referencing a Jeremy Wilmot clip): impressive, but not something he's rushing to try himself — for him the appeal of foiling is turning average, unremarkable days into fun ones, so deliberately seeking out barrels felt like the wrong risk/reward trade (paraphrased).
