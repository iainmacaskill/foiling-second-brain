# S - Ep 001 An Introduction

The show's debut. [[Liam]] and [[Freddie]] introduce themselves and explain what [[The Generic Foiling Podcast]] is: self-deprecatingly "a vanity project / ego-driven drivel," but genuinely an attempt to share what they chat about on their car-shares to the beach. The bulk of the episode then walks through a recent surf-foiling trip to North Cornwall.

## In brief
Two Devon-based watersports-industry mates who kite, wing, foil and surf across "many disciplines" decide to record the conversations they already have. They pitch the show as covering *all* foiling disciplines because — their central claim — foiling is uniquely "techie," generating far more gear-and-design chat than any watersport they've done before. Most of the episode is a debrief of three surf-foil sessions at Constantine Bay and what those taught them about gear, conditions and expectation.

## Key topics & claims
- **Why the show exists (both hosts):** it grew out of their car-sharing conversations travelling around [[Exmouth]], Devon and Cornwall to chase conditions. Both work in watersports full-time — Liam on the **teaching/retail** side (a shop on the Exmouth seafront), Freddie on the **gear distribution / new-product** side (a sales rep) — so they have access to gear and industry knowledge they want to share. A stated "pipe dream" is that a modest audience earns them demo gear from UK distributors to review.
- **The "generic" angle (Liam):** both kitesurfed for 15+ years and had "those conversations to death"; the foiling disciplines feel new and exciting, and — his repeated claim — foiling has a "techie" side (appreciation of design, gear, conditions) that he hasn't seen in other watersports.
- **How they got into foiling:** Liam took a few years out of kiting and "missed the introduction of foiling completely," then got into kitefoiling early through the people he works with — back when you couldn't buy off-the-shelf kitefoil gear. He notes he "made the wrong purchase" first time and it briefly put him off (flagged as a topic to revisit). Freddie had a hiatus from kiting, rediscovered the passion, and found wing foiling suited his existing love of riding waves/swell with a wind-power device.
- **The Cornwall surf-foil trip (main content):** a midweek overnight near Polzeath/Padstow; three sessions at **Constantine Bay** (split into the "Constantine" and "Boobies" sides). Day 1 morning was 3–4ft and "more than either of us had surfed in before" — they felt outgunned. Liam was on a **high-aspect, low-/reduced-chord wing** that wouldn't lift and "spat him out"; he could barely get a glide. Session 2 he switched to a **beginner wing** (bigger, lower stall speed, more forgiving) and had a transformative session — pumped easily and his mood flipped instantly. Session 3 (repeat conditions) Freddie stepped up from his 1080 to the 1480 for easier take-offs.
- **Technique/gear reasoning (Liam):** a larger-chord, chunkier surf wing has a much lower stall speed, so it "gives you time to think," gets going in smaller waves, and is more forgiving — versus the very specific pumping technique the razor-thin high-aspect wings demand.
- **Expectation & goal-setting (Liam, coaching mindset):** he goes into hard sessions counting any wave as a bonus and does a "pre-session assessment" of conditions vs objectives; Freddie's motivation is heavily tied to how the previous session went. Liam ties this to teaching kitesurfers who get demoralised on their first solo sessions — "count the victories."
- **Surf-foil etiquette:** sharing waves with surfers, staying out of the way, saying hello; they point to the **Kerno Foil Crew**'s written surf-foil etiquette guide (transcribed "cologne foil crew" — clearly *Kerno Foil Crew*).
