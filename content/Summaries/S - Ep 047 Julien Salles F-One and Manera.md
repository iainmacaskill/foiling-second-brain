# S - Ep 047 Julien Salles F-One and Manera

[[Julien Salles]] — son of [[F-One]] founder [[Raphaël Salles]], newly appointed General Manager as of summer 2023, and head of sister brand [[Manera]] — sits down for a wide-ranging, unusually candid family-and-business episode: how F-One was bootstrapped, how Julien's role evolved from reluctant teenager to GM, and a long technical run through masts, foils and wings. Recorded remotely (Julien at home in Montpellier, "the office is too noisy"); [[Liam]] and [[Freddie]] recorded it before a Portugal trip.

## In brief
F-One was founded from nothing by Julien's parents — his dad [[Raphaël Salles]] (ex-windsurf world tour) doing R&D/marketing, his mum running finance, HR and admin, both self-described as "50% of the company." Julien avoided the family business on principle as a teenager (mountain biking instead), joined F-One at 23 as a marketing assistant, ended up building Manera into an accessories brand, and became F-One's GM this past summer, with his dad still testing product but stepping back from day-to-day office responsibilities. The episode ranges across F-One's foil line-up (Skate, Seven Seas, Phantom and a confirmed Phantom V2), a mast recall story, the mechanics of a rare pattern-sharing deal with [[Duotone]], hard-vs-soft wing handles, wing durability R&D, and Julien's own downwind obsession — plus the long-running "Manera Socks" joke, which turns out to be a real product in development.

## Key topics & claims
- **Origin story (Julien):** F-One started "from pretty much nothing out of their bedroom" when Julien was six; early on his parents "had to steal some carton box from the local markets" to pack boards because they couldn't afford packaging — what his mum calls "the potato years." F-One remains 100% family-owned with no outside investors, which his parents are "really proud" of.
- **Julien's path:** kited from age 10–11 but wasn't passionate about water sports until 20 — mountain bike downhill/slopestyle was his escape from being "the entrepreneur's/windsurfing star's son." Studied a business bachelor's (SME management), deliberately avoided joining F-One, then took a 6-month marketing-assistant contract at 23 and never left. Took over the then-neglected Manera, becoming its product developer, then brand manager, then F-One GM "since this summer" (2023) — a promotion he says he never formally asked for.
- **Manera's positioning (Julien):** deliberately staying a "small challenger" brand rather than chasing scale like Rip Curl/Boardriders-style names; product-first, not marketing-first. ~70% of Manera's turnover is neoprene, ~50% of that in surfing; Manera is roughly 10% of F-One's total business, strong in California/US. The famous "socks" bit is real: Manera is designing socks (his in-house designer reportedly unimpressed at being pulled off F-One work for it) because Julien "needed socks and didn't want to buy them" — 10,000 pairs, prototypes due "next week," launch pending feedback (the hosts are offered first UK testing rights).
- **Deflator/downwind accessories (Julien):** Manera is developing two bags for the deflator (parawing-style) downwind method — a hydration-style bag doubling as general wing security kit (~2 weeks from release) and a dedicated deflator bag with drain holes and a pocket for pump/flashlight/safety gear, still in development. Small hand pumps (à la early two-line kite pumps) are already available (e.g. Decathlon); Julien says this product line won't make money but "we have to develop the sport."
- **Kiting still matters (Julien):** despite marketing focus shifting to wing/foil, F-One's turnover is roughly split into thirds across wing foil, kiting and other categories — "we are a water sports company, we're not a kite surfing company." R&D is done in-house in Montpellier by ~6–7 dedicated testers per category, supplemented by daily rider feedback (Julien mentions a tester referred to as "kiahi" — name uncertain, possibly garbled — who emails feedback every morning).
- **Foil range (Julien):** Skate is F-One's best-selling foil ever, only ~6 months old at time of recording, high-performance and fast but harder to control in stormy chop. Seven Seas (Julien's own go-to for small bumps) is a downwind-oriented foil with far more glide than the Phantom, which he calls a "beginner to intermediate" foil that "makes you look good" and is easier to control in messy conditions. A **Phantom V2** is confirmed as coming, roughly a year to eighteen months out, aimed at a slower, more controllable foil for stormy conditions — Julien argues there's room in the range for both "fast foil and slower foil."
- **Mast recall (Julien):** F-One's HM mast (14mm, €1,500) and standard carbon mast (16mm, €1,000) are both still made — Julien says the HM outsells the standard despite the price, "people understand how important a good mast is." A production run (Julien is vague on numbers) of the newer slim/HM mast had to be recalled after F-One's long-term factory partner deviated from the specified carbon layup and the masts broke in testing; F-One redid the production run with the correct layup at the same factory rather than switching suppliers, calling it "a trustful partner" relationship. QC process: in-water testing with riders who "break everything," plus lab testing of first-off-the-line production pieces.
- **Rider feedback anecdote (Julien):** when F-One sent a set to Australian rider Adam Bennett (name as best transcribed; not otherwise verified in the vault), his first reaction was praise for the mast, not the foil — "your mast is incredible, I'm going to ride your foil" — which Julien cites as proof a mast can matter as much as the foil itself.
- **Hard vs soft wing handles (Julien):** F-One has stuck with soft handles since day one ("we sell what we ride") but is adding a third wing model alongside the Strike and Swing — a comfort/cruising-oriented wing — that will ship with soft handles standard but offer optional hard handles as a spare part across all three models, driven by distributor pressure (UK distributor Dom is namechecked) rather than F-One's own preference.
- **Wing durability (Julien):** durability is one of F-One's top three purchase considerations for customers. Contrary to a popular belief that canopy material is the main durability issue, Julien says it's actually the leading edge — as it wears/bends, the trailing edge becomes floppy — so R&D (including the "High-Tex" leading-edge/strut material) has focused there rather than on canopy fabric.
- **The Duotone pattern-sharing story (Julien):** in what Julien frames as a rare move, F-One shared a wing pattern with [[Duotone]] for their Ventus/CWC-style product, for a modest commission — done "to protect ourselves" (so factories can't copy the design freely) rather than to make money, and framed as "healthy competition" between respected rivals.
- **Downwinding as personal passion (Julien):** says downwinding is, for him, better than pure surfing — "for the first time in my life I felt something that is better than pure surfing," a rare claim given how he describes surfing as historically "the king of water sports." He calls the deflator method the most accessible on-ramp for beginners (short, controlled sessions of a known distance rather than committing to a full downwind run) and describes F-One's new Jam wing series — a 1900 for dock-starts (releasing the week of recording) and a 1600 coming later that winter, aspect ratio ~9–10 — as purpose-built for learning to downwind.
- **Comparing brands (Julien):** asked what he'd ride if not F-One, he singles out [[Lift Foils]] (respects their early lead on extreme aspect ratio and monoblock fuselage design, even if not his personal riding style) and [[AFS Foils]] (surprised and impressed by their Silk and Pure foils). On [[Armstrong]] and [[AXIS Foils]], he notes both have gone toward high-camber downwind foils, a design direction F-One tried and stepped away from — "not really our design philosophy."
- **Facebook community aside:** Julien follows the F-One Wingfoil Club Facebook group (~4,000 members) and says customers there often know more about the product range than F-One's own staff. Both hosts and Julien separately bring up [[Adrian Roper]] as active and generous (if occasionally teasing) on a foil-building Facebook group.
- **Cabrinha rider question:** asked (via a listener question) whether Cabrinha paid F-One to take on a departing rider (name transcribed as "T2 Knobbid" — garbled, unverified), Julien says there was no payment; the rider wanted to focus on racing rather than freestyle/competition and the two parties "were looking for something else."
- **Hood River/AWSI (Julien):** calls the Hood River AWSI trade event his favourite of the year and a highlight of "the best industry ever" for its collegial vibe between competing brands; mentions hearing the local shop sold 200 downwind foil boards in a single year.
