# Wing Foiling

Riding a hydrofoil board while holding a handheld inflatable wing for power. It is the discipline the show returns to most — the hosts' home discipline and the "keep it Generic" default (Ep 108). *General framing: winging is the most accessible entry point to foiling.*

- The hosts trace their own winging progression across the catalogue — early careers in Ep 3, low-volume "sinker" wing boards in Ep 14, home-spot sessions in [[Exmouth]] (Ep 14, Ep 115).
- **Both learned around late 2020** (Ep 3): [[Liam]] first sent out by [[Steph Bridge]] on an [[F-One]] Swing; [[Freddie]] via an Andy Gratwick demo course on an [[Ozone]] Wasp. Shared lesson: don't drop board volume / foil size too fast — buy for what you're trying to achieve and keep a crossover period (a push-back on industry "willy-waving" over small boards).
- Strap philosophy: front-strap vs strapless is a recurring host disagreement; by 2026 Liam is moving toward **strapless** as parawing reduces his exposure to gnarly white-water sections (Ep 3, Ep 147).
- Recurring gear debates: [[Front Wings]] high vs mid aspect (Ep 7), [[Boards]] volume, and wing brands like [[Slingshot]], [[F-One]], [[Duotone]] and [[Ozone]].
- Coaching perspective from [[Rob Claisse]] on kite & wing progression (Ep 20).

## Ripple additions (batch 2, 2026-07-20)

- Keahi De Aboitiz (Ep 6) rates wing foiling as the discipline closest to tow foiling, prized for letting you catch a wave up to 30 seconds before it breaks; his go-to in lighter wind or lull conditions.
- Mike Raper (North, Ep 24) on North's wing range: Nova (freeride, v3 2023 redesign), SF Surf (prone/wing-surf), HA/Swivel (high-aspect), MA (mid-aspect), Nova Light Wind (paired with a large hydrofoil for sub-white-capping conditions — hosts pushed back skeptically).
- Klaas Voget (Duotone, Ep 73) traces winging's early adoption to a Tenerife dealer meeting (early 2019) where Duotone's wing designer showed prototypes; the same trip produced the first Fanatic 'Skywing' wingboard.
- Dave Kalama (Ep 142): short 'crossover' boards aimed at pump-wingers hinder beginner progression; found winging felt like windsurfing ('mowing the lawn') and lost interest.
