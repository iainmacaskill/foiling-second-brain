# Parawing

A compact, packable soft wing (no rigid frame or boom) used to get a foiler up onto swell, then stowed to ride downwind — a fast-rising discipline/gear category across the show's 2025 episodes. Sometimes written "Parawang" by the hosts (Ep 137).

- Design insight from makers: [[Ewan Jaspan]] on the [[NAISH]] parawing (Ep 123), [[Gabriel Verrier-Paquette]] on [[Slingshot]] parawing/wing design (Ep 119).
- Framed repeatedly against downwind SUP as a rival way to catch bumps (Ep 108, Ep 109); state-of-the-market rundown from [[Gav Blake]] (Ep 105).
- Heavy parawing use reported at the [[Hood River]] AWSI trips (Ep 116).
- **2026 market read** ([[Freddie]], an [[Ozone]] UK rep, Ep 147): parawing is where nearly all his sales are — Ozone's **Pocket Rocket V2** outselling the V1 and back-ordered — while **wing sales have "dropped off a cliff."** He half-dreads the **F1 Frigate V2** launch pulling attention away. Open question in the trade: does parawing need *lessons* or only *coaching*, and does adding it just "water down" what a school can teach rather than grow the customer base.
- Its rise is blamed for **killing off downwind SUP paddle foiling "for the masses"** (Ep 147) and for shifting the hosts away from wave-hunting spots like [[Bantham]].

## Ripple additions (batch 2, 2026-07-20)

- Ep 110 (Tiesda You, Starboard): scepticism-to-interest arc on parawing; Hood River locals swapping downwind boards for parawings; currently an accessory to downwind foiling rather than its own discipline.
- Ep 47 (Julien Salles, F-One / Manera): Manera's two deflator bags in development — a hydration-style bag and a dedicated deflator bag with drain holes/pump pocket.
- Ep 142 (Dave Kalama): parawing pulled Maui paddlers away from paddle-downwind last summer but a migration back (mixing both) is now underway.
- Ep 123 (Ewan Jaspan, NAISH) — thorough design detail: bridle-length vs stowability trade-off (NAISH targets one-arm-length lines vs Ozone's longer-bridle approach); canopy material trade-offs (nylon/paragliding-style fabric for tear resistance/packability vs more water-resistant but less tear-resistant alternatives); danger of under-tuned prototypes (front-canopy collapse at power above ~20 knots); sizing/tuning across rider weights (~50kg to 100kg+). NAISH's 'Morph' was roughly the third power wing to market after 'BRM Flow'.
