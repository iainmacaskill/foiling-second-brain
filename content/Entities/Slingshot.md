# Slingshot

Foil, wing and board brand with kiteboarding roots — the single most-featured gear maker across the catalogue, covered from the top down and across its design team.

- CEO/founder [[Jeff Logosz]] on where Slingshot came from and is going (Ep 127); the ONELOCK mast & fuselage system with product designer [[Bobby Johnston]] (Ep 102); wing/parawing/board design with [[Gabriel Verrier-Paquette]] (Ep 119).
- Riders/ambassadors: [[Fred Hope]] (Ep 84, and the "Project Drag" board programme Ep 144) and [[Sam Light]], marketing lead in the UK (Ep 41).
- [[Edo Tanas]] joins Slingshot from [[F-One]] (Ep 139). The hosts visited Slingshot's [[Hood River]] offices (Ep 84, Ep 127).
- **Project Drag** (Ep 144): a collaborative board-and-mast programme built around a **Tuttle ("total") connection**, handing design to outside shapers [[Ken Adgate]] and [[Kyle Touhey]] plus a Tuttle-box builder ("Michael"). Notable for a public **Discord** and factory-level transparency, a compressed ~6-month dev cycle, paying to industrialise the Tuttle box for other brands (Hydrocraft already buying), and a 17mm high-stiffness mast in development. Pitched at the top ~10–20% of riders; ~$1,800 boards. [[Mikes Lab|Mike Zajcik]] and [[Johnny Heineken]] fed back informally.
- 2026 downwind racing (Ep 147): new signing [[Edo Tanas]] **won the season's first race on [[F4]] foils**, not Slingshot's (they hadn't developed a downwind foil in time) — and Slingshot released a candid video saying so, which split community opinion.
- **Origin of OneLock** (Ep 84, [[Fred Hope]]'s first sit-down, recorded at Slingshot's [[Hood River]] office during [[AWSI]]): the screwless connection started ~3 years earlier as a fix for two practical problems — corrosion on the older **Phantasm** aluminium fuselage (dependent on the factory's aluminium QC) and fiddly beach setup for less dexterous/older customers. Only once in development did the team realise how much drag the screws were adding, which pushed the geometry toward a fully streamlined, non-bulbous foil. Getting the carbon collar thin enough cost roughly 50 broken masts and several expensive mold retools; first physical sample came 1.5–2 years before Ep 84.
- Current track system is **RTS (Rapid Track System)** — front teeth plus rear flags — which Fred says still adds drag versus OneLock; he expects plate/track systems generally to start disappearing within ~3 years but says a shared cross-brand standard needs industry-wide buy-in, not a single brand acting alone (Ep 84).
- 2024 product notes (Ep 84): **Slingwing V5** (building on the NXT), **Cod V2/NXT** in the Big Air new-school series, and the **Laser** — a light-wind wingboard that doubles as a downwind board, with tail volume deliberately kept clear of the mast to preserve pumping. Fred names the minimalist Hobie/Hopecraft **Joystick** and **UFO** kite-foiling gear as what he's proudest of alongside OneLock.
- The **OneLock** name itself was a deliberated internal decision built from two concept "columns" — security and speed — with Slipstream, Impossible, Fast Latch and Quick Lock rejected along the way (Ep 84).
