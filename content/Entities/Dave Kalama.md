# Dave Kalama

Legendary waterman who, to the hosts' disbelief, turned out to be a semi-regular listener and asked to come on (Ep 142) — introduced via [[Gabriella Bella]]. One of the show's marquee guest bookings.

- Ep 142: Maui-based board shaper and one of the earliest hydrofoil pioneers (self-credited only with "the original version" of foiling; credits "Alex" of Go Foil, surname not given, with making the sport accessible). Toe-surfed for *Die Another Day* alongside [[Laird Hamilton]], and did stunt/support work on a Molson beer commercial and the surf film *In God's Hands* (which he pulled out of over the script).
- Design philosophy: prioritises efficiency-to-foil above all else in a board; sees himself as a problem-solving "engineer" rather than an artist, self-taught with no formal design training. Believes big-brand design is constrained by economics/risk-aversion in a way his small operation isn't.
- Created the **Barracuda** and later the **Gator** board shapes — the Gator's blunt nose came from extensive (60–70 run) blind scale-model testing that disproved his own assumption that a pointed nose was faster. Now rides Gator exclusively; smallest is the Gator Compact (winging/pump-wing/prone).
- Has switched from a lifetime on Go Foil to riding [[F4]] (Orca, mainly the 685) for downwind; has been downwinding for close to 10 years.
- Developed and is still testing a magnetic on-body-to-on-board weight-transfer system, arguing (heavily caveated) that added weight improves high-speed foil control on open-ocean Maui downwinders even though lightness helps getting up on foil — contrary to prevailing industry "lighter is always better" orthodoxy.
- Has come around against Tuttle ("total") mast/box connections, mainly over limited fore/aft adjustability versus a plate system — picked Tuttle boxes for his Room 101. This is a direct counterpoint to [[Fred Hope]]'s pro-Tuttle **Project Drag** pitch on [[Slingshot|Ep 144]].
- Runs no marketing department; relies on word-of-mouth. Business is small relative to majors like [[Armstrong]] but the Gator has "kept me in business" through a difficult US market.
- View on beginner gear: short "crossover" boards aimed at pump-wingers hinder beginners' progression; recommends longer boards while learning fundamentals.
- On [[Parawing]]: tried winging early and found it felt like windsurfing, lost interest; sees the parawing as a better fit for downwinding and is taking it up partly to bring his smaller 530cm² foil back into rotation.
