# NAISH

Kite, wing and foil brand, covered through [[Ewan Jaspan]], its parawing and kite lead designer (Ep 123). The episode centres on [[NAISH]]'s [[Parawing]] and Ewan's long career as a pro kiteboarder and freestyle pioneer.

- **Ownership (Ep 123):** Sold roughly two and a half years before the episode (so ~2023) by its former owner — referred to only as "Robbie" in-episode, very likely co-founder Robby Naish but unconfirmed — to "Kubus" (name transcription-uncertain), NAISH's long-standing European distributor.
- **Team restructure (Ep 123):** Previously almost entirely based in one Maui building; now spread globally — [[Ewan Jaspan]] (kite + parawing design) in [[Hood River]]; a foil/wing designer ("Noa") and a newly-joined downwind-foil specialist ("Andrew Gibbons," ex-[[Lift Foils]]) in Maui; kite R&D ("Dez") also Maui; a windsurf specialist split between Maui and Spain; R&D/management leadership now in Holland.
- **Parawing programme (Ep 123):** First-generation power wing "the Morph" (launched earlier in 2025), designed by an engineer/paraglider ("Matt," since departed) as roughly the third such wing to market. Ewan took over the follow-up design this past summer; development emphasised safe power-up behaviour after an early prototype proved prone to dangerous front-canopy collapse, and short, one-arm-length bridle lines for easier stowing, at some cost to line-angle efficiency versus longer-bridle rivals like [[Ozone]].
- **Materials (Ep 123):** Parawing canopies use nylon paragliding-style fabric (referenced as "Skytex 32" from a French supplier heard as "Porcher," transcription-uncertain) for tear resistance and packability, at the cost of a shorter lifespan than kite/wing canopies (coating wear, water absorption); NAISH was in early talks with materials suppliers about more durable alternatives. Kites use a ripstop "Quad Tex" canopy which Ewan argues gives kites much longer service life than wings or parawings.
- **Custom/niche product (Ep 123):** Built a 2.2m single-strut kite originally for a hard-riding Maui test rider (name transcribed "JD Follow"/"JD Fallow," uncertain), later sold in small limited runs — popular in Maui, slower elsewhere.
